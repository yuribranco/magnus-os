# Review Checklist — Auditing Existing Copy with BA

Use this when the user drops a headline, ad, VSL script, landing page, or email in front of you and asks for feedback (explicitly or implicitly). Go through the sections in order. Don't jump to tactics before you've diagnosed.

## Phase 1 — Triangulate the market (before judging any word)

Answer these three questions BEFORE reading the copy critically. Write them down.

- [ ] **Mass desire:** In one sentence, what pre-existing hope/fear/craving is this copy trying to channel? Is it a Permanent Force (instinct, chronic problem) or a Force of Change (trend, style)?
- [ ] **Awareness level (1–5):** Based on the market this copy targets (not what the copy *claims* to target), where is the prospect?
  - 1. Most Aware — knows product, ready to buy
  - 2. Product Aware — knows product exists, not convinced
  - 3. Solution Aware — wants the outcome, doesn't know your product
  - 4. Problem Aware — feels the problem, no named solution
  - 5. Unaware — problem is vague, unspoken, or taboo
- [ ] **Sophistication stage (1–5):** Based on the competitive landscape (how many similar claims has this market already heard?):
  - 1. First — no similar products
  - 2. Second — enlarged claims
  - 3. Third — new mechanisms
  - 4. Fourth — mechanism elaboration
  - 5. Fifth — exhausted, identification required
- [ ] **Is the copy actually written to the level you identified?** Very often it's written one or two levels above the real market (talking about the product when the prospect is still at Problem Aware). This is the single most common failure.

## Phase 2 — Headline diagnostic

- [ ] **Does the headline stop a stranger?** Schwartz: "Your headline has only one job — to stop your prospect and compel him to read the second sentence of your ad."
- [ ] **One thought, not two?** Single-thought rule: if the first thought holds, they read the second. If you stack two, both lose.
- [ ] **Matches the awareness level?**
  - Level 1 → product name + offer
  - Level 2 → product + superiority
  - Level 3 → desire/solution, not product
  - Level 4 → problem, not product or desire
  - Level 5 → identification, not product/price/function/desire
- [ ] **Matches the sophistication stage?**
  - Stage 1 → bare claim
  - Stage 2 → enlarged claim
  - Stage 3 → new mechanism leading
  - Stage 4 → elaborated mechanism
  - Stage 5 → identification
- [ ] **Is the headline an Echo Ad?** Does it sound like a swap-and-replace of a past winner? If you can mentally substitute another product name without changing anything else, it's an echo.
- [ ] **Is the verbalization sharp?** If flat, run `headline-operations.md` and pick 3–5 operations that match the angle.
- [ ] **Does the headline promise more than one benefit?** If yes, cut it down to one. Every extra benefit dilutes the hook.

## Phase 3 — Body copy diagnostic

- [ ] **Does paragraph 2 force the reader to read paragraph 3?** Each sentence has one job: compel the next. Test this sentence by sentence on the first 3–5 paragraphs.
- [ ] **Is the dominant desire being INTENSIFIED?** Does the body paint the world of fulfillment in concrete, vivid scenes — not abstract benefits?
- [ ] **Does the reader get to IDENTIFY with the role the product confers?** Status, belonging, self-image. "You become the kind of person who…"
- [ ] **Is there GRADUALIZATION?** Is the reader led from where they are now to where the product takes them, step by step — so each step feels inevitable? Or are there leaps that require trust the copy hasn't earned yet?
- [ ] **Are existing beliefs being REDEFINED where they block the sale?** Or is the copy arguing head-on with beliefs (which never works)?
- [ ] **Is there a MECHANIZATION — a concrete "how it works"?** Not "our advanced formula" — actual, inspectable detail. Especially required at sophistication Stage 3+.
- [ ] **CONCENTRATION: is everything subordinated to one dominant theme?** Or does the copy switch themes mid-stream?
- [ ] **CAMOUFLAGE: could this be mistaken for content the prospect actually wanted to read** (a story, a guide, a news item)? Or does it announce "I am an ad" in the first line?

## Phase 4 — Proof, believability, specificity

- [ ] **Every claim documented?** Numbers, names, times, places, mechanisms. Not "amazing results" but "lost 37 pounds in 12 weeks."
- [ ] **Specificity over generality?** "In 2 seconds, Bayer begins to dissolve in your glass" > "fast-acting aspirin."
- [ ] **Believability check:** would a skeptical prospect mentally discount this by 50%? If yes, the claim is over-promised; add a mechanism or a limitation to ground it.
- [ ] **Is proof at the RIGHT weight for the sophistication stage?**
  - Stage 1–2: testimonials, before/after, direct claims work.
  - Stage 3+: you need mechanism-level proof. Testimonials alone are suspect.

## Phase 5 — Offer, value stack, close

- [ ] **Is the offer anchored against something concrete?** "$X in value, your price is $Y" with each component priced. Without anchoring, any price is arbitrary.
- [ ] **Is the risk addressed?** Guarantee, refund, reversal. "If this doesn't do X, we don't want your money."
- [ ] **Is the close a real two-option choice?** The strongest Option 1 is *inaction* (going back to the current painful state), not a straw-man competitor. See the Persimmon VSL close as a working example.
- [ ] **Does the CTA name a single next action?** Not three options. One.

## Phase 6 — Rationalizations that mean "go back to Phase 1"

These phrases in your own assessment mean you're rationalizing past a diagnostic failure. Stop and re-run Phase 1.

| You catch yourself saying | It actually means |
|---|---|
| "The copy is fine, they just need to tweak the CTA" | You haven't located the awareness level; the whole headline is probably wrong |
| "It just needs punchier language" | Sophistication-stage mismatch; you need a new mechanism, not better adjectives |
| "Add a testimonial section" | Stage 3+ market; testimonials alone won't break through |
| "Make the hook stronger" | The hook isn't the problem — the angle is. Go back to mass desire |
| "Try a question headline" | You're reaching for operations without having decided the angle |
| "I think the tone should be more playful" | Tone is downstream of positioning. Taste talk usually hides structural failure |

## Output format when you finish the review

Return a structured report. Keep it in the user's working language. Verbatim quotes from the copy under review stay in their original language.

```
## Diagnosis
Mass desire: <1 sentence>
Awareness: <level + evidence from the copy/market>
Sophistication: <stage + evidence from competitors>

## What's wrong (in priority order)
1. <structural issue — usually an awareness/sophistication mismatch>
2. <second-order issue — proof, believability, single-thought violation>
3. <tactical issue — verbalization, operation choice>

## What's right (keep these)
- <things the copy is doing well, specifically>

## Recommended moves
1. <the single biggest lever — usually changing the angle>
2. <body copy technique(s) to apply>
3. <headline operations to try, with 2–3 sample rewrites>

## The one thing to do next
<single next action, concrete>
```

## When the copy is a VSL

Add a VSL-specific check: does the script follow the LHPSO frame (Lead → História → Problema → Solução → Oferta)? Each section has a distinct job:

- **Lead (0:00–1:30, ~225 words)** — sell the content. Create an information gap. Give the viewer a reason to watch the next 12 minutes. Not descriptive, curiosity-driven.
- **História (1:30–4:30, ~450 words)** — sell hope and trust. Background (credibility) → Emotional (connection, specific character) → Discovery (transition to thesis). ABT framework.
- **Problema (4:30–7:30, ~450 words)** — problem mechanism. Agitate progressively. Name the enemy. End with "It's not your fault" reframe.
- **Solução (7:30–10:30, ~450 words)** — present your mechanism. Quality-first. Complete infrastructure. Why your incentive structure is different.
- **Oferta (10:30–14:00, ~525 words)** — value stack → price anchor → proof → guarantee → two-option close. Option 1 should be inaction, not a competitor.

If a VSL is flat, the most common failures are: (1) Lead is descriptive instead of curiosity-driven, (2) no História section at all, (3) Problema is informational instead of agitating, (4) no value stack in the Oferta, (5) close uses a straw-man Option 1 instead of inaction. See `~/Documents/Persimmon/funil-de-vsl/docs/briefings/intro-to-aesthetics/vsl-script.md` appendix for a worked example of these 7 failure modes and their revisions.
