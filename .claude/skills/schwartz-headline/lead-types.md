# Lead Types — Eugene Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising*

The 4 lead types this skill covers, in the order they appear in the grid output.

Schwartz não cataloga "4 lead types" com esses nomes exatos no livro. O que ele faz é descrever, em Capítulos 2 (Awareness) e 3 (Sophistication), **padrões estruturais de abertura** que aparecem de novo e de novo. Esta taxonomia destila esses padrões em 4 leads operacionais — cada um citando verbatim a passagem onde Schwartz descreve o padrão.

Source: `~/Downloads/breakthrough-advertising.pdf` — chapters cited inline below.

---

## 1. Promise Lead

- **Verbatim Schwartz (Ch. 2 — Stage 2 da Awareness, "The Customer Knows of the Product But Doesn't Yet Want It"):** "In all seven cases, the approach is the same. You display the name of the product—either in the headline or in an equally large logo—and use the remainder of the headline to point out its superiority. The body of the ad is then an elaboration of that superiority—including visualization, documentation, and mechanization."
- **Quando usar:** Mercados Solution-aware, Product-aware e Most-aware, onde o leitor já reconhece a categoria de solução e responde a uma promessa clara e mensurável. Schwartz: "Your headline—in fact, your entire ad—need state little more except the name of your product and a bargain price."
- **Estrutura:** `[Benefício específico]` em `[tempo / condição]` `[sem obstáculo conhecido]`. Schwartz usa a "raw promise" como base — Ch. 3 (Sophistication Stage 1): "the promise itself is paramount."
- **Exemplo do livro (Ch. 3 — Stage 1 da Sophistication):** "I'D WALK A MILE FOR A CAMEL!" e "CHESTERFIELD—THEY SATISFY!" — Schwartz comenta: "This raw promise of enjoyment gradually became elaborated and embellished to push it to the limits of believability."
- **O que evitar:**
  - Promise vazia sem mecanismo em mercado Stage 3–5 (saturado). Schwartz é explícito (Ch. 3): "If your market is at the stage where they've heard all the claims, in all their extremes, then mere repetition or exaggeration won't work any longer."
  - Promise em Unaware. Schwartz (Ch. 2, Stage 5): "a direct statement of what your product does, what desire it satisfies, or what problem it solves, simply will not work… you cannot mention them in your headline."

## 2. Problem-Solution Lead

- **Verbatim Schwartz (Ch. 2 — Stage 4 da Awareness, "How to Introduce Products That Solve Needs"):** "This is the problem-solving ad. It might be thought of as a special case of the desire ad mentioned above, since the technique of writing it is so similar. Here you start by naming the need and/or its solution in your headline. Then dramatize the need so vividly that the prospect realizes just how badly he needs the solution. And then present your product as the inevitable solution."
- **Quando usar:** Mercados Problem-aware (lead clássico): dramatize o problema → cristalize → prometa a solução. Também funciona em Unaware se a dor for amplamente sentida mas não-nomeada — Schwartz mostra isso no caso Postum/"WHY MEN CRACK…" (Stage 5 com gancho de problema oculto).
- **Estrutura:** Schwartz dá 3 modos: (a) só o problema — "Corns?"; (b) problema + solução juntos — "Stops maddening itch."; (c) problema + solução + remoção da limitação — "Shrinks hemorrhoids without surgery."
- **Exemplo do livro (Ch. 2, Stage 4):** "Do YOU make these mistakes in English?" — Schwartz: "the emphasis of a single word—the emotional sharpening of an already easily-identified image—provides the answer."
- **O que evitar:**
  - Pular pro produto antes de dramatizar o problema. Schwartz é claro: "dramatize the need so vividly that the prospect realizes just how badly he needs the solution" vem ANTES de "present your product as the inevitable solution."
  - Usar em Most-aware. Schwartz (Ch. 2, Stage 1 — Most-aware): "The customer knows of your product—knows what it does—knows he wants it… need state little more except the name of your product and a bargain price." Drama de problema atrasa quem já decidiu.

## 3. Big-Secret Lead

- **Verbatim Schwartz (Ch. 3 — Stage 3 da Sophistication):** "What this market needs now is a new device to make all these old claims become fresh and believable to them again. In other words, A NEW MECHANISM—a new way to making the old promise work. A different process—a fresh chance—a brand-new possibility of success where only disappointment has resulted before."
- **Quando usar:** Mercados Solution-aware e Product-aware sofisticados (Stage 3–4 de sophistication, NÃO Stage 5 — Stage 5 é identification, território do Story Lead). Claims diretos já não furam; o segredo/mecanismo novo restaura a credibilidade. Schwartz: "Here the emphasis shifts from what the product does to HOW it works. Not accomplishment, but performance becomes dominant."
- **Estrutura:** Provoca curiosidade nomeando algo desconhecido (segredo, descoberta, mecanismo escondido) → conecta esse algo ao benefício esperado pelo prospect. Schwartz mostra isso reforçado por "mystery" em Ch. 2 ("Now! Run your car without spark plugs!") e por "wonderment" ("Who ever heard of 17,000 blooms from a single plant?").
- **Exemplo do livro (Ch. 3, Stage 3 da Sophistication, em reducing field):** "FLOATS FAT RIGHT OUT OF YOUR BODY!" e "FIRST WONDER DRUG FOR REDUCING!" — Schwartz: "the promise itself is subordinated to the mechanism which accomplishes that promise. This mechanism is featured in the headline."
- **O que evitar:**
  - Segredo sem mecanismo concreto e testável. Schwartz é taxativo (Ch. 3, Stage 4): "the mechanism you use must not only be new and legitimate, but it must be accepted as believable and significant by your market." Curiosidade vazia = clickbait, e Schwartz diz que cada Stage 3-4 mal-feito "makes this problem of acceptance more and more difficult."
  - Usar em Unaware. Não dá pra prometer segredo de algo que o leitor não sabe que existe. Schwartz: em Unaware, "you cannot simply shift from one desire to another."

## 4. Story Lead

- **Verbatim Schwartz (Ch. 2 — Stage 5 da Awareness, sobre os exemplos narrativos clássicos):** "In the 1920s most of the great ads were narrative stories—either first-person confessions, or third person revelations, or comic strips dramatizations… while the narrative style of both is now old-hat, you can still respond to their power. Both tap desires that still exist… and both evoke those desires, and channel those desires, so effectively, that if they were rewritten in today's idiom, and applied to different products, they still might sell millions of dollars' worth of goods today."
- **Quando usar:** Unaware e Problem-aware. Schwartz dedica o Stage 5 inteiro a esse lead — quando "the prospect is either not aware of his desire or his need—or he won't honestly admit it to himself without being lead into it by your ad—or the need is so general and amorphous that it resists being summed up in a single headline—or it's a secret that just can't be verbalized." A história funciona porque o leitor consome a narrativa antes de perceber que é venda.
- **Estrutura:** Schwartz nomeia o padrão como "Projecting an Ultimate Triumph That the Prospect Will Identify With" (e variantes: "Projecting the Result of a Problem…", "Giving Words to a Hidden Dream", "Exploiting a Hidden Fear"). A regra-âncora dele: "Your prospect must identify with your headline before he can buy from it. It must be his headline, his problem, his state of mind at that particular moment."
- **Exemplo do livro (Ch. 2, Stage 5):** "THEY LAUGHED WHEN I SAT DOWN AT THE PIANO. BUT WHEN I STARTED TO PLAY!—" (U.S. School of Music) e "OFTEN A BRIDESMAID BUT NEVER A BRIDE" (Listerine) — em ambos Schwartz mostra que o lead é narrativo de personagem específico → situação concreta → virada/descoberta → ponte pro produto.
- **O que evitar:**
  - História sem virada — vira diário, não vende. Schwartz: "It cannot simply be a startler, or an attention-getter, or humorous, or cute. Nor can it mask the fact that it has no headline behind a pretty picture. Most copy writers use a fifth stage problem to write an empty headline, and are therefore simply wasting their client's money."
  - Usar em Most-aware. Schwartz (Ch. 2, Stage 1 — Most-aware): nesse mercado o "headline can do the full selling job" — eles querem oferta, não warm-up narrativo.

## Cruzamento Lead × Awareness (cheat sheet usado pela skill em runtime)

| Lead | Awareness onde brilha | Awareness onde falha |
|---|---|---|
| Promise          | Solution / Product / Most         | Unaware |
| Problem-Solution | Problem-aware                     | Most-aware |
| Big-Secret       | Solution / Product (sofisticado)  | Unaware |
| Story            | Unaware / Problem-aware           | Most-aware |

A skill usa essa tabela em runtime pra marcar "cells forçadas" — combinações `awareness × lead` que estão na coluna "falha". Essas cells ainda são geradas (pra completar o grid 5×4), mas a skill sinaliza no rodapé que aquela combinação é não-natural e deve ser usada com cautela.
